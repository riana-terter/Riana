Deep Dive · Demand Planning Card

Transforming Demand Planning through Design Thinking

A full end-to-end redesign: from a legacy, table-based tool to a visual, graph-driven forecasting experience.

Design Thinking Journey

Before · Pain Points · What We Changed

Before

Legacy · table-based · rigid

Key Pain Points

  • Complex tabular configurations, high error risk
  • No visibility of data trends at a glance
  • Hidden options, poor navigation
  • Weak information architecture
  • No distinction between basic and advanced users

What We Changed

  • Timeline-based visual design replacing tables
  • ML model selection built into the interface
  • Configuration separated from mass treatment
  • Organised by usage frequency & user profile
  • Advanced tools for experts; simple defaults for all

Process in action

Discovery

Persona & experience map, built in workshops with Professional Services, Architects & Customer Success.

Ideation

Crazy 8s and Design Studio sessions converged on a hybrid visual approach for basic and advanced users.

Prototype

High-fidelity prototypes with realistic data — for stakeholder sign-off, developer handoff, and user testing.

Design System

One shared system kept all 7+ modules consistent across teams in France and China.

Case Study

Designing for Global Telecom Leaders

At Cisco's Design Studio, I collaborated with cross-functional teams across Europe, Asia, and the USA to deliver tailored TV and media experiences for Liberty Global and Vodafone — two of the world's largest telecom operators.

Liberty Global — The Challenge

  • A set-top box product matured over 4 years, yet with usability and practicality issues in key features
  • Country managers across 7 markets reporting user dissatisfaction with certain functionalities
  • Missing features — VOD and Replay TV — were reducing the product's competitiveness

Process

Client Collaboration

Monthly trips to Amsterdam for reviews, feedback collection, and roadmap discussions. Built trust by actively listening to country managers and addressing regional user needs.

Design Tailoring

Analysed feedback to refine existing features. Aligned future features (VOD, Replay) with Liberty Global's specific goals, ensuring scalability across all 7 countries.

Cross-Cultural Bridge

Facilitated collaboration between Dutch client teams and Cisco's French Design Studio, resolving misalignments and establishing a lasting working model.

Team Coordination

Led a cross-functional squad of PM, designer, product owner, and technical leader. Indirectly managed graphic and technical designers to align with Liberty Global's standards.

Impact

Significantly increased user satisfaction, validated by positive feedback from country managers across 7 markets

Transformed a tense partnership into a long-term collaborative relationship — improving Cisco's reputation within Liberty Global

Introduced VOD and Replay TV features, boosting adoption and market competitiveness

My Process

Design Thinking in Practice

Not a checklist, but a way of working. Open a step to see how it translated into real decisions at Sunstice and at Cisco.

01DiscoveryGetting to the real problem+
  • At Sunstice: Ran workshops with Professional Services — the team that knew where users actually struggled. Discovered the Demand Planning card wasn't just slow: it was causing misconfiguration.
  • At Cisco: Monthly trips to Amsterdam to hear Liberty Global country managers describe regional user dissatisfaction first-hand — not filtered through a PM.
02IdeationBreaking out of the obvious solution+
  • At Sunstice: Crazy 8s sessions stopped the team from just "fixing the table" — 8 minutes, 8 sketches, engineers and PS in the room, forced divergence before convergence.
  • Design Studio brought UX, tech, and business into one room; the outcome was a shift from table-first to graph-first — a fundamentally different mental model for forecasting.
03PrototypingMaking it real before building it+
  • At Sunstice and Cisco: High-fidelity prototypes with real supply chain data, not dummy content. Stakeholders could feel the difference immediately.
  • Investor pitch: Built a future-vision Bloom prototype that helped secure Cathay Capital and Sagard — design work that directly drove business outcomes.
04Feedback LoopsIterating until it works in real life+
  • At Sunstice: Professional Services reviewed every cycle and translated real user friction into design input — not abstract requirements.
  • At Cisco: Monthly Amsterdam reviews caught cultural friction early. We iterated until Gartner users wrote, unprompted: "intuitive, powerful, fully configurable."
05Delivery & ImpactMeasuring what actually changed+
  • +35% adoption across 7+ modules · +30% usability improvement on the Demand Planning Card across all user profiles.
  • Design work cited in Gartner peer reviews — rare for UX to be named specifically by clients.
06LeadershipBuilding the conditions for good product work+
  • Mentored up to 7 designers and integrators — making sure the process was taught, not just applied.
  • At Cisco: bridged the Dutch client and the French design studio. At Infor: first designer on SyteLine, championing UX in an engineering-driven culture.
